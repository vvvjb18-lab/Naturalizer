I want to get better at being academic by defeating my constant procrastination. The issue 
of procrastination was persistent in my life since primary school. Profuse procrastination 
regarding academic related things could pose severe psychological trauma, acute anxiety and 
worsening academic outcomes (Sirois & Pychyl, 2013; Tice & Baumeister, 1997). It is 
crucial to conquer procrastination and adopt a planned, active learning pattern to achieve my 
personal growth.
In order to set goals to conquer my procrastination, first I will first explain my own 
experiences and poor study behaviours. In my primary school period, my parents were 
hoping me to do well in school and they payed very much attention to my homework. My 
parents always give me extreme punishments whenever I have some minor mistakes or didn't 
get 100% marks in exams. This made me felt scared and anxious. So under the mad pressure 
of the fam, i just procrastinate on doing my homework/revision till the last minute so i 
wouldn't have to face the stress of doing something wrongly. If I look back to my childhood 
period, I can see that my parents practice authoritarian type of parenting which lead me to 
suffer from the problem of procrastination. Authoritarian parents are very demanding and 
controlling so it is very likely for children to be become nervous, susceptible to stress and 
afraid of failure (Baumrind, 1971). Finally, Wood (2013) points out that our self-concept is 
greatly influenced by a process called reflected appraisal, i.e. the way in which we think 
important others evaluate and see us. Through the process of internalization of my parents 
evaluation of me, I has linked my self-worth with 100% grades. Therefore, procrastination is 
the way for me to protect my self-esteem. If I procrastinate something, in case of bad 
outcome I can always say that it is because of the lack of time instead of the lack of ability. 
Through this, my external locus of control become more rooted (Hanna et al., 2010).

Well no fluffy promises....instead i've planned some self-monitoring tools which meet the 
"SMART" test to develop a studious approach & eliminate last minute cramming for the next
month
As to 'S'(Specific) criteria, I will divide all following assignments into 3 subtasks, specifically
analyzing the question and thinking about the idea to build a outline, making draft and finally
doing final proofreading. By applying this aim-setting method specifically, I will not feel 
burdened by arduous academic tasks. As to 'M'(Measureable) criteria, I will keep on revising 
exam for 6 hours a week, that is from 7 pm to 9 pm, 2 hours for each session. on Tuesday, 
Thursday and Saturday. I'm going to keep track of my study progress using Yeolpumta app. It
will give me the details of how long I studied and what I learned. In order to test my 
understanding, I will solve a set of past papers. If i get less than 70% accuracy, the next week 
i will spend extra time working on these weak topics. For "A" (achievable), I will have a 
quiet place set up at home for these regular scheduled fix-up sessions. The function of 
blocking application in Yeolpumta app is useful to avoid being distracted by digital devices. 
In order to make sure I'm consistent, if I happen to have some sort of un-avoidable event, I'll 
push it off to the next night (so it'll be this coming evening) to stay on track for the week. As 
to 'R'(Relevant) criteria, by controlling my regular study plan, my internal locus of control 
and positive self-concept will be enhanced. It proves that academic achievement is subject to 
consistent personal efforts (Hanna et al., 2010). As to 'T'(Time-bound) criteria, I will firmly 
go on with such structured plan in 1 month. and check my progress in every week. With this 
method, I can form a sustainable studying habit instead of falling into last-minute cramming.
