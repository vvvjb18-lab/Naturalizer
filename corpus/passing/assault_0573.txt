Compare and Contrast the Business Efficiency of Physical Stores and E-
commerce Platforms
The modern business environment is highly competitive. Businesses need to 
utilise strategies to entice customers while managing costs to run their business 
operations. Physical stores are traditionally the prime channel for selling 
products. Customers are able to see and buy the products in person. Digital 
technology has evolved allowing e-commerce platforms to be an important 
alternative channel. Businesses are no longer confined to physical stores. They 
have the opportunity to sell products online. While these two channels share the 
objective of driving sales and meeting customer demands, they have different 
ways of utilising resources, delivering products and the operations of the 
business. Physical stores typically require shop space, staff and stock kept in the 
area. A physical store usually requires a shop, staff and in-store stock. E-
commerce is more reliant on digital infrastructure, warehouses and delivery 
systems. Ultimately, it is not that one channel is more efficient than the other as 
there are many factors to consider such as product features, customer 
behaviour, logistics, etc. The aim of this essay is to discuss and contrast the 
efficiency of business between physical stores and e-commerce platforms. This 
will involve looking at the cost of operations and inventory management. Also, 
how they are able to deliver a convenient and prompt service to customers. 
Physical stores vs e-commerce platforms Cost of operations Inventory 
Management Convenient and Prompt Service
One of the biggest differences between physical shops and e-commerce is in the 
operation cost and inventory. For a physical store, there are costs of rent, 
electricity, salaries, maintenance of retail space. These costs may add to the 
burden of businesses, especially if there is a low flow of customers. On the other 
hand, e-commerce can reach customers without the need for a large number of 
traditional stores, which may lower some of the property costs. But, of course, 
online businesses also have to pay for website upkeep, digital infrastructure, 
warehouses, packaging and shipping. As Li et al. (2015). pointed out, the 
effectiveness of online and physical retailing may be influenced by inventory
