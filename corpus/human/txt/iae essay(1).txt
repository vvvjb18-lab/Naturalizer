Describe the cause and effect of illegal immigration 
 
The history of our nation is a history of immigration ( Obama., 2013) . This 
statement highlights the crucial role of immigrants, including unauthorised ones, in the 
nation’s ongoing development. For the longest time, the annual immigrant limit was set 
at 675,000. Over time, strict policies there led to  a sharp decline in the immigrant 
population. However, policies such as U.S. immigrant law and citizenship regulations 
have significant ly altered the population structure. In the past few years, many 
unauthorised immigrants have gained legal status through family reunification. Passel 
and Krogstad (2024) note that the illegal immigrant population grew from 2019 to 2022. 
The United States Citizenshi p and Immigration Service (USCIS) addresses illegal 
immigration and protects the rights of legal immigrants, he lping them integrate 
smoothly into society. Despite the global nature of undocumented immigration, many 
people remain unfamiliar with the issue. Therefore, this essay explores the causes of 
illegal immigration  driven by family and econom ic pressures, discusses  how 
unauthorised immigrants can reduce labour cost s and stimulate the economy,  and 
examines why an excessive influx of illegal immigrants  can overwhelm public 
resources. 
 
Firstly, significant family and economic pressures  are a key cause of illegal 
immigration. According to Passel et al., (2024), the number of unauthorised immigrants 
from Mexico , Guatemala, and Honduras has increased significantly. These regions 
suffer from high crime rates, severe poverty, and a lack of economic opportunities, with 
poor salaries and high taxes unable to sustain a basic standard of living. This compels 
many illegal immigrants to seek employment abroad. The author adds that the average 

wage for U.S. production workers is approximately nine times higher than that in 
Mexico, providing a strong economic incentive for immigration. Additionally, family 
considerations and concern for the next generation drive immigrants. The pursuit of 
U.S. citizenship is motivated by access to superior educational resources and quality 
healthcare. Many families flee their home countries due to war and political persecution 
in search of a safer and more stable environment. Consequently, many illegal 
immigrants aspire to have U.S.-born children and join legally present family members, 
resulting in a sharp increase in illegal immigrants. 
 
Secondly, a positive effect of unauthorised immigrants is their potential to reduce 
labour costs and boost the economy. Peri et al. , (2015) explain that authorised 
immigrants contribute to  a low-cost labour force, often  due to  their lower skill and 
educational levels. They work in low-technology industries such as agriculture, 
construction, and services, filling vacancies in the local labo ur market and supporting 
the operation of these industries  (Casarico.,2015). The presence of low-skilled 
unauthorised immigrants helps reduce labour costs for employers, which encourages 
companies to create more jobs and boosts employment opportunities for native workers. 
Undocumented immigrants could contribute $1.7 trillion to the economy over the next 
decade (Zaiour., 2021). Casarico (2015) explains that illegal immigrants, like other 
residents, need to buy food, clothing, housing and daily necessities. Their local 
consumption activities directly promote the development of retail, catering and service 
industries. This consumer demand increases the demand for goods and services and 
promotes economic vitality. 
 

On the other hand, illegal immigrants can also exert significant pressure on social 
resources. Almasi (2019) notes that unlawful immigration affects not only 
redistribution expenditures but also public order spending, which decreases funding for 
welfare. The authors further highlight that a large influx of illegal immigrants and their 
children entering the public school system increases the burden on the education system. 
This increased demand can strain resources and diminish the quality of education, 
particularly in communities that are already resource constrained. Furthermore, this 
influx also dilutes the medical resources available to domestic residents, reducing their 
ability to obtain medical services. According to Camarota et al. , (2024), illegal 
immigrants often have lower levels of education and income, resulting in lower tax 
contributions relative to the public services they use. For instance, in the United States, 
while illegal immigrants have high work rates and pay taxes, their low average earnings 
result in greater reliance on government services than the tax contributions they provide. 
This creates a net fiscal deficit in public resources. 
 
In conclusion, illegal immigration is primarily driven by family and economic  
pressures. It provides employment opportunities and  boosts the econ omy by offering 
low-cost labour. However, it also places a significant  strain on public resources and 
services. To sum up, while illegal immigration improv es employment opportunities, 
further research is necessary to understand the costs of integrating all illegal immigrants. 
Possible solutions should be offered to help integrate their rights into society.
