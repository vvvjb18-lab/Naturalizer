Evaluate the impact of artificial intelligence on education

Artificial intelligence tools such as ChatGPT have become new challengers for traditional teaching, given their unexpected influence on classrooms and coursework. ChatGPT attracted 100 million users within two months of its launch in 2022, and students were among the earliest and heaviest adopters of artificial intelligence (UNESCO, 2023). Not only can artificial intelligence personalise learning and reduce teachers' marking workload, artificial intelligence can also weaken the assessment system that education has depended on for centuries. Although many educators welcomed the efficiency brought by artificial intelligence tools, many worried that students would gradually outsource their thinking to machines. Therefore, this essay will analyze the influence of artificial intelligence on education in three aspects: the personalisation of learning, the relief of teachers' workload, and the threat to academic integrity.

Firstly, artificial intelligence can personalise learning at a scale that no school could previously afford. Bloom (1984) found that students who received one-to-one tutoring performed around two standard deviations better than those taught in a conventional classroom, a gap that has been described as the two-sigma problem ever since. In practical terms, an average student with a personal tutor would outperform roughly 98% of classmates taught in the ordinary way. Human tutors, however, are far too expensive for most public schools to hire in sufficient numbers. Holmes et al. (2019) explained that adaptive learning systems driven by artificial intelligence can imitate parts of this tutoring relationship by adjusting the difficulty and sequence of exercises according to each student's answers, giving weaker learners unlimited patient practice at minimal cost. Hence, artificial intelligence has the potential to narrow the long-standing achievement gap between privileged and underprivileged students, provided that schools guarantee equal access to devices.

Secondly, the use of artificial intelligence can relieve teachers' routine workload. Teachers currently spend a large portion of their evenings and weekends marking assignments, writing feedback and preparing worksheets, and this invisible burden is a major reason for the teaching profession's high dropout rate in many countries. Automated marking systems built on artificial intelligence can complete routine grading within seconds, releasing teachers' energy for mentoring, lesson design and classroom interaction (UNESCO, 2023). For instance, when a community college in the United States adopted artificial intelligence feedback on early drafts, instructors reported that they could finally hold individual writing conferences with every student during the semester. Thus, artificial intelligence does not replace teachers but returns teachers' time to the tasks that software cannot handle, such as motivation and discipline.

However, the rise of artificial intelligence threatens the integrity of assessment. Turnitin (2024) reported that among over 200 million student papers screened since 2023, around 11% contained at least 20% artificial-intelligence-generated content, forcing institutions to rethink what a submitted essay can still prove about its author. The deeper danger goes far beyond the limits of detection software. If students habitually ask chatbots to produce their first draft, the practice of constructing arguments from evidence, which education is supposed to train, would quietly disappear from their daily routine. Some universities in Australia have already restored pen-and-paper examinations in response to artificial intelligence plagiarism. In addition, educational platforms collect sensitive data on children's behaviour and performance, and the long-term storage of such records remains loosely supervised in many countries (UNESCO, 2023). Under this situation, the convenience of artificial intelligence would be obtained at the cost of honesty and privacy.

To conclude, this essay has outlined three influences of artificial intelligence on education. Artificial intelligence not only can personalise learning for students at different levels, but artificial intelligence can also take over routine marking so that teachers concentrate on genuine instruction. However, the spread of machine-written assignments is undermining the credibility of coursework, and the collection of student data raises privacy concerns that schools are not yet equipped to manage. Overall, artificial intelligence is a powerful assistant rather than a replacement for teachers, but institutions need to redesign their assessment methods and supervision rules before the benefits of artificial intelligence can be enjoyed safely.
