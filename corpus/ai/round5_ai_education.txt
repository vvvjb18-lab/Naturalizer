Evaluate the impact of artificial intelligence on education

Artificial intelligence tools such as ChatGPT have become new challengers for traditional teaching, given their unexpected influence on classrooms. ChatGPT attracted 100 million users within two months of its launch in 2022, and students were among the earliest and heaviest adopters (UNESCO, 2023). Not only can AI systems personalise learning and reduce teachers' workload, they can also weaken the assessment system that education depends on. Although many educators welcomed the efficiency brought by these tools, many worried that students would outsource their thinking to machines. Therefore, this essay will analyze the influence of artificial intelligence on education in three aspects: the personalisation of learning, the relief of teachers' workload, and the threat to academic integrity.

Firstly, artificial intelligence can personalise learning at a scale that no school could previously afford. Bloom (1984) found that students who received one-to-one tutoring performed around two standard deviations better than those taught in a conventional classroom, a gap that has been described as the two-sigma problem. Human tutors, however, are far too expensive for most schools to provide. Holmes et al. (2019) explained that adaptive systems can now imitate parts of this tutoring by adjusting the difficulty of exercises according to each student's answers, giving weaker learners unlimited patient practice at minimal cost. Hence, AI has the potential to narrow the long-standing gap between privileged and underprivileged students.

Secondly, the use of artificial intelligence can relieve teachers' workload. Teachers currently spend a large portion of their evenings marking assignments and preparing materials, and this burden is a major reason for the profession's high dropout rate. Automated marking and feedback systems can complete routine grading within seconds, releasing teachers' energy for mentoring and lesson design (UNESCO, 2023). For instance, when a college in the United States adopted AI-assisted feedback on drafts, instructors reported that they could finally hold individual conferences with every student during the semester. Thus, the technology does not replace teachers but returns their time to the tasks that machines cannot handle.

However, the rise of artificial intelligence threatens the integrity of assessment. Turnitin (2024) reported that among over 200 million student papers screened since 2023, around 11% contained at least 20% AI-generated content, forcing institutions to rethink what a submitted essay can prove about its author. The danger goes beyond detection. If students habitually ask a chatbot for their first draft, the practice of constructing arguments, which education is supposed to train, would quietly disappear from their daily routine. In addition, educational platforms collect sensitive data on children's behaviour and performance, and the long-term use of such records remains loosely supervised in many countries (UNESCO, 2023). Under this situation, the convenience of AI would be obtained at the cost of honesty and privacy.

To conclude, this essay has outlined three influences of artificial intelligence on education. It not only can personalise learning for students at different levels, but it can also take over routine marking so that teachers focus on genuine instruction. However, the spread of machine-written assignments is undermining the credibility of coursework, and the collection of student data raises privacy concerns that schools are not yet equipped to manage. Overall, artificial intelligence is a powerful assistant rather than a replacement for education, but institutions need to redesign assessment and supervision before the technology's benefits can be enjoyed safely.
